My conceptual framework

Who gets exposed, and who pays?

Rawls imagined two lotteries: the lottery of birth — who your parents are — and the lottery of life, the shocks along the way. Sociology shows they are rigged together. The first lottery decides both who gets exposed to the second and who pays when it hits. My research measures that rigging causally, and asks what policy can do about it.

Prevalence

Selection into risk

Is a disadvantage more common among children of low-status families because of their origin — or because of who ends up there? Identified through traits exogenous to family background, such as sex or month of birth, and through institutional rules.

Penalty

Moderation of the penalty

When the shock lands, do advantaged families absorb it — compensatory advantage — or convert it into a wider edge? And through which mechanism: expectations, information, time, money?

Theory evaluation

Evaluation, not one coefficient

Each theory is turned into several observable implications, confronted with rivals and with data, and judged on substantive size, robustness and mechanism — not on a single significant estimate.

Nothing in the engine is specific to education. Any stratified risk — a legal status, a technology, an environment — fits the same template.

The framework comes out of my doctoral thesis, Selection or moderation? (European University Institute, 2023), where I tested it on three shocks: grade retention in Spain, boyhood in the United Kingdom and school entry age in Germany. Read the thesis.

Forthcoming lines of research

Two years inside the state produced the reforms, the instruments and the data infrastructure. These are the three lines they make possible — one engine, three risks.

Line 1

Legal status, family stability & intergenerational inequality

Spain’s 2026 extraordinary regularisation is a once-in-a-generation reform, and I helped design the analytical baseline for it. Legal status is a mark administered by the state; removing it should reduce employer uncertainty — a treatment on the second moment of statistical discrimination, not only on its mean.

  • Observe: registers
  • Listen: conversational data
  • Experiment: take-up RCT
Line 2

Credentials, mismatch & the value of education

One in two migrant workers in Spain is overqualified for their job. The fast-track recognition of foreign qualifications rolls out staggered across sectors and regions — an event study waiting to happen, and a rare chance to referee three theories that usually only argue.

  • Positional value
  • Signalling
  • Human capital
Line 3

AI, task restructuring & stratification

Exposure indices ask what AI can do. EPOCH asks what will still be asked of humans, scoring occupations on empathy, presence, judgement, creativity and vision. I built its Spanish application inside government; the academic step is to formalise it and take it to trajectories.

  • Measurement: EPOCH-ES
  • Selection into exposure
  • Institutions as moderator

Selectivity and risks across educational transitions in Europe

Espadafor, M. C., Bernardi, F., Lievore, I. & Valdés, M. T.

MapIneq Working Paper D3.3 · Horizon Europe

What the paper does

Harmonised European microdata for eight countries. Among those who do not make the transition to tertiary education, parental education cushions the risk of ending in a low-status occupation — moderation, now on labour-market ground. Where transitions are more selective, the cushion matters more.

Education

The uneven risk and effect of grade retention across family origins

Espadafor, M. C.

What the paper does

Spain runs one of the OECD's most retention-intensive systems. Gender and birth month — traits exogenous to family background — identify who is exposed; an instrument built from regional retention rules identifies the penalty. Low-SES students face both a higher risk of repeating and a larger penalty when they do: exposed below, sheltered above.

Education

Are boys more vulnerable to family disadvantage? Evidence from the United Kingdom

Espadafor, M. C.

What the paper does

UK cohort data through the sixth-form transition. Sex at birth is not sorted by family background, so exposure is clean by design. High-SES families moderate the male penalty at the transition — net of prior cognitive and non-cognitive skills. The male disadvantage in education is a class phenomenon.

Education

Does family socioeconomic status compensate for an early entry into school life? Evidence from Germany

Espadafor, M. C. & Passaretta, G.

What the paper does

German cohort data, entry age made quasi-random by birth-month rules, two independent identification strategies that converge. There is a clear early-entry penalty and no compensation by family background — the honest null that gives the framework its scope conditions.

Foresight2024–2025

Generaciones del Futuro

Spain's first birth-cohort longitudinal study

Register-based sampling, linkage between administrative records and surveys, intergenerational by design. I conceived the idea, designed the linkage across existing surveys and registers, led the international expert meetings and secured the tri-party State agreement between the Foresight Office, INE and CSIC. Governance modelled on the Millennium Cohort Study, NEPS and the PSID.

Lead author of the chapter on labour, education and welfare.

Education2025

The intergenerational effect of educational expansion: evidence from a natural experiment in Spain

Espadafor, M. C. & García-Sierra, A.

European Sociological Review

Spain's 1970 reform, which raised the compulsory school-leaving age from 12 to 14, as an instrument for parental education. Each additional parental year raises children's upper-secondary attainment by 10 percentage points — but the compliers are the mildly disadvantaged, not the poorest, which is why overall equality did not move.

Teacher bias by student SES or measurement error in ability? A cautionary tale for observational studies

Gil-Hernández, C. J. & Espadafor, M. C.

Social Science Research

Residual teacher bias by family background shrinks substantially once ability is measured with repeated low-stakes tests rather than a noisy snapshot. What survives sits on low-performing high-SES students: compensation through evaluations, not blanket favouritism.

Can a low emission zone improve academic performance? Evidence from a natural experiment in the city of Madrid

Valdés, M. T., Espadafor, M. C. & Conte Keivabu, R.

Population & Environment, 47

Madrid Central as a natural experiment on university-entrance scores, 2015–2022, with difference-in-differences, synthetic DiD and an event study. The zone raised scores; NO₂ fell where scores rose, and school intake stayed stable across the boundary.

Education2023

Selection or moderation? Three essays on educational inequalities

Espadafor, M. C.

Doctoral thesis, European University Institute

Three shocks, one engine: grade retention in Spain, boyhood in the United Kingdom, school entry age in Germany. Each chapter asks whether exposure is socially stratified and whether the penalty is absorbed unequally — and the German chapter reports the honest null that gives the framework its boundaries.

Supervisor: Fabrizio Bernardi

The negative consequences of sports gambling opportunities on human capital formation: evidence from Spain

Espadafor, M. & Martínez, S.

PLOS ONE, 16(10), e0258857

An original geolocated panel of Madrid high schools and betting-shop openings. Proximity lowers performance, cafés show no such effect, and public schools in poorer areas are hit hardest — an environmental exposure that widens inequality of opportunity.
